Original fitSimulator

The simulator moves the original curve down by 1.372 mm. This rebase is unvalidated: it changes absolute height at every load. The points on these plots are calculated, not raw bench samples.

01 / MEASUREMENT

Height from load

The stored coefficients imply an exponential height law. The original sample table and regression procedure are absent from the inspected files.

h_original(m) = 27.351 + 1.372 exp(0.7669 m) mm

m is equivalent mass in kg. At m = 0, the original fit is 28.723 mm high.

02 / REFERENCE CHANGE

Start at the seal

The model needs zero force at its sealed rest state. Subtracting 1.372 mm creates a zero-extension origin.

d = 1.372 expm1(F / B) mm h_sim = 27.351 + d mm

B = g / 0.7669 ≈ 12.78739 N. The 28 mm design height remains a separate packaging value.

03 / SIMULATION

Force from motion

Invert the exponential and evaluate it at each cup’s axial displacement. The 3D view here shows the static elastic branch.

F = B log1p(d / 1.372) N k = B / (1.372 + d) N/mm

The simulator additionally includes provisional damping, compression, shear and failure laws.

At the fit boundary the rebased model predicts 50.696 mm total height; at pull-off it predicts 60.351 mm. These are calculated heights, not measured travel limits. Free height is not a calibrated extension stop.

What happens at failure?

The simulation detaches at tensile force ≥ 41.18793 N. It does not keep carrying the threshold force after separation. In this explorer, the load remains as a threshold marker while the separated cup returns to its illustrative free shape.

Beyond 3.77 kg the curve is extrapolated. Dynamic damping can reach pull-off sooner. Shear and peeling can detach a cup before the axial threshold is reached.

What remains provisional?

Default damping: 8 N·s/m. Compression stiffness: 12 N/mm. Shear stiffness: 4 N/mm. Friction: 0.5. Peel angle: 20°. Sliding limit: 5 mm. These are model inputs, not measured material properties.

No leakage, pressure dynamics, cardboard flex, hysteresis or physical rubber travel limit is solved. Optional pressure scaling is a hypothesis; VMECA curves are separate similarity estimates.
